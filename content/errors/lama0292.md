# LAMA0292: The scope of a member doesn't match the scope of its type

| | |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | Error |
| **Reported by** | Metalama.Framework |
| **Message** | `Execution scope mismatch: the member '<Member>' is <MemberScope>, but the declaring type '<DeclaringType>' is <DeclaringTypeScope>. Change the scope of the declaring type, for example with the [CompileTime] or [RunTimeOrCompileTime] attribute, or move the member to a type of a compatible scope.` |

## Cause

Metalama assigns a scope to every declaration: run-time, compile-time, or run-time-or-compile-time. A type and its members are compiled into the same assemblies, so the scope of a member must be compatible with the scope of its type. For instance, a compile-time member can't live in a run-time type, because the run-time type doesn't exist in the compile-time assembly.

Metalama reports LAMA0292 when it finds one of the following combinations. Templates are exempt, because they're legitimately run-time members of compile-time types.

* A compile-time member, typically a member marked with `[CompileTime]`, in a run-time type.
* A run-time-or-compile-time member, for instance a member marked with `[RunTimeOrCompileTime]`, in a run-time type.
* A run-time member in a compile-time type.
* A method of a run-time type that isn't marked with a scope attribute but has a parameter of a compile-time-only type, like `IMethod`. The message then says `compile-time (inferred from parameter types)`.

In the message, `<Member>` and `<MemberScope>` are the member and its scope, and `<DeclaringType>` and `<DeclaringTypeScope>` are the declaring type and its scope.

## Resolution

Make the scope of the type and of its members consistent:

* When the whole type is meant to run at compile time, move the scope attribute from the member to the declaring type, as in the following example.
* When the type must keep its current scope, move the member to another type that has the same scope as the member.
* For a method that has compile-time parameters in a run-time type, mark the type with `[CompileTime]`, or move the method to a compile-time type.
